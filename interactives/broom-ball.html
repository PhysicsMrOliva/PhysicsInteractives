Broom ball force lab

A bowling ball rolls on a gym floor. A broom push is the only horizontal force, so the ball only speeds up, slows down or changes direction while the broom is touching it. Set the pushes, press Play, and connect each force to the ball’s motion.

Lab video

The original broom ball video. When “Move the simulation with the video” is on, playing or scrubbing the video moves the animation and graphs to the same time, so you can compare the real ball with the model. Gray dots on the graphs are the position data from the video (velocities are worked out from neighboring points).

The data file labels positions in meters. The ball covers about 58 of these units, so if your gym is smaller than that, the data was probably calibrated in a different unit; the shapes of the graphs are what matter here.

Video analysis & graphing

Record a motion on video, track it in Sigmund to get position-time data, and graph and fit that data in nPlot. Both run in the browser on iPads, Chromebooks, Macs and laptops.

The tools

Sigmund: video analysis

Load a video, calibrate it with an object of known length, set t = 0 and the origin, then click on the object frame by frame to collect position-time data. Copy the data straight into nPlot or download it as a CSV file.

Works best on an iPad, Chromebook or laptop; a phone screen is too small to use it comfortably.

Open Sigmund

nPlot: graphing and curve fitting

Paste or type data, label both axes, and choose a model such as linear, quadratic or square law. The Tangent Finder gives the slope at any point, and Copy Slopes turns a fitted position-time graph into velocity-time data.

Save graphs in the browser or to a file, and copy them into a lab report.

From video to graph

The basic workflow for a video analysis lab.

  1. Record the video

    Hold the camera still, perpendicular to the motion, with a meter stick or other known length in the frame. See the tips below.

  2. Load and calibrate

    In Sigmund, load the video, then click Calibrate and draw a line along your known length and enter its length and units.

  3. Set the time and origin

    Step to the frame where you want to start and click t = 0. Then set where x = 0 and y = 0 are.

  4. Get data points

    Click Get Data Points and click the center of the object in each frame. Sigmund advances the video for you after each click.

  5. Copy the data

    Click “x vs. t” or “y vs. t” to copy two columns, or Download CSV. Then click Launch nPlot.

  6. Graph and fit

    Paste into nPlot and label both axes (the graph appears once both are labeled). Choose the model that matches the shape of the data, and compare models using the RMSE.

If the motion has several sections, like the broom ball lab, don’t fit one model to the whole graph. Identify each section of constant velocity or constant acceleration first.

Tips for recording a good lab video

  • Use the “Most Compatible” (H.264) video format if Sigmund says your video can’t be played. Newer phones save in a high-efficiency format by default.
  • Don’t edit or trim the video on your phone. Editing scrambles the timing information Sigmund needs. If you want a short video, record a short video.
  • Keep it short: under 2 minutes and under 300 MB.
  • Skip slow motion unless the motion is very fast. If you use it, set the frame rate manually in Sigmund.
  • Put the known length in the same plane as the motion, and keep the camera still and square to it.
